# Packets per second with 64-byte frames

How many of the smallest frames can a box forward a second **without losing one**? This page is what a G8S, a T12S, a Q16 and an HL1 measured with the flow table on (the factory settings) and off (an optimised set) (2026-11-06, 11-09 and 11-16; firmware 6.6.261104 on the G8S and T12S, 7.6.261106 on the Q16, 7.6.261112 on the HL1). Packets per second is the limit that small-packet traffic runs into: the same pps carries more throughput as packets grow, so the 64-byte pps times the average packet size of real traffic is the box's ceiling for that traffic (0.92 Mpps × 600 bytes ≈ 4.4 Gbit/s, say).

## Method

- **The box under test generates nothing itself.** Another GRISM's traffic-gen input sends the frames over real cables into the box under test, which forwards them through one chain (one filter, `ip.src == 11.0.0.0/8`, matching everything) to another port, cabled back to the generating box, which counts them. No LOOP ports.
- **Frames**: UDP, `packet_size` 60, which is exactly 64 bytes on the wire with the FCS (traffic-gen's `packet_size` excludes the FCS; 64 would make 68-byte frames). Each generator cycles through a contiguous range of source addresses (`src_ip_min` / `src_ip_max` / `src_ip_inc`), so every frame is a different flow.
- **Flows**: with the flow table on, the generators' source addresses together come to about **75%** of the table's capacity (`flowCacheBaseSize × 16`, times the number of work cores on the ARM64 line): 480,000 on the G8S, 1.8 million on the T12S, 6.35 million on the Q16, 1.93 million on the HL1. Too few is wrong -- a single flow is held on one core by its atomic tag on OCTEON, which measures one core rather than the box (0.49 Mpps for a single flow on the G8S), and a few tens of thousands leave the table nearly empty and the figures high; too many is wrong too -- a nearly full table pulls them down (see the notes after the results). With the flow table off the flow count does not matter.
- **Rate**: the first trial is unpaced (line rate, or the generator's limit); if that drops, traffic-gen's `speed` is bisected, each trial 15 s of warm-up and a 60 s window, seven trials (about 1% resolution).
- **Pass**: over the 60 s the box under test has 0 `inDrops`, `inErrors` and `outDrops` on **every port** (a Q16's NIC drops are counted on the engine's uplink ports P0-P2, not on the V ports), and its out count equals its in count (within a tenth of a second of traffic: the in count is the hardware's, the out count a sum of per-core software counters, and they are not read at one instant). The difference between what the generating box sent and got back (inPackets + inDrops, since its own engine drops at the port what it cannot absorb) only fails a trial past 1%, as a guard against loss nobody counts (a switch, a cable). **The HL1 is the exception**: near its limit it loses frames that no drop counter shows (see the notes after the results), so for it what went missing is what counts -- frames taken in and neither sent nor counted as dropped, and the generating box's sent-minus-got-back -- and more than about 5 ms of traffic (15,000-20,000 frames in 60 s) fails the trial.
- **Flow on** (factory) = the settings after "Reset to factory settings (management address kept)", nothing changed (MEC on for the MEC models). **Flow off** (optimised) = the flow / flowv6 tables, L7 parsing, tunnel inner parsing, SCTP / TCP / QUIC reassembly, IP fragment correlation, deduplication, MEC (s1cCorrelation and the GTP tunnel header), GRE / GTP / VXLAN correlation and coreSession all off, measured after a reboot; everything else as shipped.
- "1 in 1 out" is one generator and one chain; "2 in 2 out" two generators and two chains (the two 10G cables each used in both directions). The HL1 has one data port, so its "1 in 1 out" is in and back out of the same 100G port, with 1, 2 and 4 generators on the Q16's V13.

- 64-byte line rate is 1.488 Mpps on a 1G port, 14.88 Mpps on a 10G port and 148.8 Mpps on a 100G port.
- **G8S**: about 0.4 Mpps with the flow table on, a limit of the whole engine rather than of a port -- 2 in 2 out totals slightly less (one more port to serve); about twice that with the flow tables, L7 parsing and the rest off. Flooded at line rate it still forwards about 0.5 / 1.06 Mpps, the rest is dropped at the in port and the out port never drops.
- **T12S**: about 3 Mpps with the flow table on, the same for 1 in 1 out, 2 in 2 out and 4 in 4 out -- again the whole box's limit; 6.2-6.3 Mpps with it off, whatever the port count.
- **Q16**: 15-16 Mpps with the flow table on (2 in 2 out and 4 in 4 out alike); with it off **27 Mpps without a loss** (4 in 4 out; flooded with 37 Mpps from twelve T12S generator cores it forwards 34.5, the rest counted as inDrops on the uplink ports P0-P2). Four 10G ports carry 59.5 Mpps of 64-byte frames at line rate, so 27 Mpps is about 45% of it.
- **HL1** (one 100G port, 8 cores, 7 of them work cores), **fixed**: about **6.0 Mpps** without a loss with the flow table on (2 and 4 generators alike -- the whole engine's limit), **11.4 Mpps** with it off (4 generators; 1 and 2 generators sent 4.5 and 10.1 Mpps with nothing lost, the generators' limit); flooded with 18 Mpps it forwards 5.85 and 11.6, the rest dropped at the in port. The limit is the engine's, not the 100G port's (148.8 Mpps at 100G line rate).
- **What was fixed on the HL1 (2026-11-16).** Before, near its limit it lost frames that no drop counter showed: inDrops and outDrops stayed 0, yet fewer frames went out than came in, by exactly what the generating box did not get back (86,000 a minute at 3.6 Mpps with the flow table on). The HL1 has 8 cores and its 7 work cores shared them with Linux -- the web service, SNMP, the updater, the management-NIC and NVMe interrupts; each time Linux took a core for 4-24 ms that core's receive queue (16,384 entries) filled, and the NIC dropped what came next without counting it. Now Linux lives on CPU 0 alone (the engine's housekeeping core, which is not busy) and work cores 1-7 run nothing else: at the same 3.8 Mpps the loss went from 60,000-250,000 frames a minute to 24, and the loss-free points from 2.7-3.2 to 6.0 (flow on) and from 3.7-3.9 to 11.4 (flow off). The fix is in the firmware (engines from 2.9.51): the boot script `linux_on_cpu0.sh` moves every process and interrupt to CPU 0 (the watcher runs it again after an update; services systemd starts later are held there by `CPUAffinity=0` in `/etc/systemd/system.conf.d/10-grism-linux-cpu0.conf`), and the engine names its lcores to DPDK itself, so one started from a program pinned to CPU 0 (the web page's service restart, the watcher's crash restart) still gets every core; it awaits a release, and the earlier rows stay for comparison. The engine's own threads must be left alone: a test once unpinned them to CPUs 0-7 as well, two work cores then shared a CPU while another sat idle, and the same 3.3 Mpps lost 400,000 frames a minute.
- **Flooded, an HL1 still drops part of it uncounted**: fixed and flooded with 18 Mpps, inDrops counted 6-12 million a second, and a further 250,000-430,000 a second came in and neither went out nor were counted -- with every core at 100% the receive queues fill just the same. So whether an HL1 drops is read from inDrops *and* from in against out; that is how this page judges its passes (see the method), anything over about 5 ms of traffic failing the trial. **From engine 2.9.53 such a loss leaves a trace**: every 64th full burst a work core asks the NIC how much is waiting on its queue, and over three quarters (12,288 of 16,384 entries) it counts one on the port's `inQueueFull` and logs at most a line a minute (`receive queue over 12288 frames waiting (P0 queue 5 x11 ...) (core 6)`); the "Receive queue full" alert (pywww 1.47.8) watches that counter, raised when it grows within a 10 s sample and cleared 60 s after the last growth.
- **The Q16 measured again (2026-11-16)** under the HL1's strict criterion (more than 5 ms of traffic between in and out fails): the Q16 has **no** uncounted loss -- in and out differ by a few dozen frames at every rate, what it drops is counted on the uplink ports P0-P2, and a pass ends on 1-2 dropped frames a minute. With Linux on CPU 0 it gains about 6% with the flow table on (14.76 → 15.71) and nothing with it off (26.46 / 26.39); the engine is the limit. The Q16's cage V5 held no module this time, so three 10G pairs were used (V7, V6, V4), the third in and out of one port; three pairs and the earlier four agree.
- **The Q16 through one 100G port**: the HL1 generating (its 100G P0 → the Q16's V13, four 25G modules bonded), the Q16 taking it in on V13 and sending it to the empty cage V5 (the switch drops it there; the engine's out counter still counts), so nothing returns to the HL1. Flow off **23.65 Mpps**, about 10% under the 26.4-27.1 of three or four 10G ports; flow on with 6 generators (6.29M flows, 74% of the table) **15.29**, the same as the multi-port 15.7; with 4 generators only 4.19M flows fit (50% of the table), which is why 18.44 reads high -- an emptier flow table, not the port count.
- **How full the flow table is changes the flow-on figures**, which is why this page uses 75%. The same T12S with the flow table on: 65,000 flows (3% of the table) took one generator core's 4.26 Mpps 1 in 1 out without a drop and 3.98 2 in 2 out; at 75% 2.97 / 2.94; at 87% 2.73. G8S: 65,000 flows 0.52 / 0.49, 75% 0.43 / 0.40, 90% 0.35 / 0.29.
- **A Q16's drops are counted on the engine's uplink ports.** Flooded with 26.9 Mpps with the flow table on, the V ports still showed in = out (17.8 = 17.8) and 0 inDrops; the 540 million frames dropped were counted as inDrops on P0-P2, the 50G ports between the switch and the engine. Look at P0-P2 to see whether a Q16 drops.
- The CPU column is the system status page's figure; below saturation it says how much is left, and 100% is the step before drops. The HL1's core 1 is the exception: it gets 11% more frames than the others (the RSS table has 64 entries over 7 queues, so queue 0 gets 10 and the rest 9), finds something at every poll and never rests, and so reads 99% with one generator while the others read 65-78%; it keeps up, and when frames were lost it was the one core losing none.

## The generators' own limits

- One traffic-gen input takes one core. A G8S generates only about 0.33 Mpps a port, so it is never the generator; one T12S core fills a 1G port and does about 3.4-4.3 Mpps on a 10G port, twelve cores (three on each of four ports) 37 Mpps together; one Q16 lcore does 4.3-5.2 Mpps on one port (all 64 bytes).
- **An ARM64 box generating, with the return on its generators' port (to be avoided before engine 2.9.52).** Each traffic-gen input takes a work core, and nobody polls that core's receive queue any more: whatever RSS sends back there stays (16,384 entries a queue, each holding a packet buffer), the HL1's six generators had 98k of its 113k buffers tied up that way and could barely send -- 4 inputs offered 8.3 Mpps, 6 inputs 7.2; with the box under test sending its output elsewhere (the empty cage V5) 4 inputs offer 23.4 Mpps and 6 offer 33.7. **From engine 2.9.52 every load points the RSS table at the queues of the work cores that still receive** (the log says `port 4: RSS over rx queues 6-6, 64 table entries`): with the return into the HL1's P0, 4 inputs offer 24.1 Mpps and 6 offer 35.0, as with V5. Seven inputs on the HL1 (all eight cores) used to make the engine exit; from 2.9.52 an input past "cores minus two" is not started, the log says `input 6 (traffic-gen) is not started: 8 cores leave room for 6 ...`, the others run, and `validate` points it out before the submit.
- Two generators on one port: fine on the MIPS64 line (the PKO queue is locked, each input on its own core); on the ARM64 line only safe when the port has a tx queue per core. A Q16's generator inputs run on lcores 1, 2, ..., each on the tx queue of its number, and its uplinks have 23 queues, so however many inputs share a V port each has its own queue: the T12S tests kept to one a port to be safe, the HL1 tests put 2 and 4 on V13 (10.1 and 18.2 Mpps together).
- What `speed` means: packets a second ≈ 131072 × speed ÷ packet_size (about 2184 × speed at 64 bytes); 0 is unpaced.
